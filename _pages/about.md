I am a PhD candidate in Translation and Interpreting Studies at the School of English Studies, Shanghai International Studies University (supervised by Professor Xiao Weiqing). My dissertation examines how viewers process errors in raw machine-translated subtitles, using EEG and eye-tracking. I am also a certified translator and interpreter, holding the **CATTI Level 1 Translator** certificate (Chinese–English, the highest tier) and the **CATTI Level 2 Consecutive Interpreter** certificate.

Motivation
======
My interest in subtitles goes back to growing up in Chongqing, the city where the monorail passes through a residential building and which TikTok turned into a celebrity. A place that wants the world to know its name puts English subtitles on everything: documentaries, ads, short videos. I grew up reading them. Later I subtitled the city's own promotional videos and learned the hard way how little time, how little space, and how much local culture you have to fit into two short lines.

The questions I study now took shape while volunteering for **TED Translators**, transcribing, translating, and reviewing subtitles. I kept running into the same kind of accident: the automatic transcription mishears a line, the machine then translates the mishearing *faithfully*, and if nobody along the chain notices, a plausible-looking error ends up on screen as fact. When the talk is about health, that is not a small thing. So I started asking what happens at the other end: when a video platform serves **raw machine-translated subtitles** with no human touch, how do viewers actually receive them? Can they tell something is off? And what makes that easier or harder?

Those questions became my dissertation. Beyond it, I also follow how AI is reshaping translation practice and translation pedagogy. Having been a translation student and then a working translator, I know the pain points of learning, especially self-directed practice: with no teacher in the room, timely and useful feedback matters enormously. So I built tools of my own: [GoInterpreter](https://gointerpreter.tech/), an AI tutoring system for interpreter trainees, and [译了么](https://ylm.life/), a practice platform for student translators.

Vibe coding
======
I build working tools for the problems I study and teach:
